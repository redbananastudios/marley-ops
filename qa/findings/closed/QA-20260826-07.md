## Summary
The per-brand card-payments switch is a dead control. `brands.card_payments_enabled` is admin-editable in Settings and is persisted, but it is read by neither the copy that claims to be gated on it nor the code that actually gates the card channel — so the toggle and the live behaviour can disagree in both directions, and flipping it changes nothing.

## Repro
Direction A — the toggle is inert:
1. Settings > Brands, flip a non-default brand's "Card payments" ON. `sanitizeBrandUpdate` persists `card_payments_enabled = true`; the card reads "Card payments on".
2. Expected: that brand's payment copy starts offering card.
3. Actual: nothing changes anywhere. `lib/quote/chase.ts:234` still emits "You can pay by bank transfer from your quote page:", `quote-email`'s `depositStepCopy` still emits "Pay by bank transfer straight after accepting", the storage-invoice bank block is still headed "Pay by bank transfer", and the Zoho commitment/balance invoice notes (`lib/quote/accept-flow.ts:167-170`, `:294-297`) still hardcode "by bank transfer (reference X) or cash".

Direction B — copy contradicts the live channel:
1. Brand toggle OFF, global `business_settings.card_payments_enabled` ON (the documented prod state).
2. `cardPaymentsAvailable()` returns true for that brand's quote, so `/q/<token>` renders the card button while every email tells the customer bank transfer is the only route.

## Evidence
`lib/comms/email-brand.ts:197` and the call sites listed above: no read of `brands.card_payments_enabled` on any copy path, and `cardPaymentsAvailable()` consults the global setting only.

## Suspected cause
The column and its Settings control shipped with the brand layer ahead of the copy/gating work that would consume it. Either wire both the copy and `cardPaymentsAvailable()` to the per-brand column, or remove the control until it does something — a persisted switch that silently does nothing is worse than an absent one, because an operator will believe they have changed the customer's payment options.

Filed `risky`: it governs how customers are told to pay, and reversing it interacts with the 2026-07-09 card-fee pricing decision — not an automation call.

## Fix (2026-08-27, `ef66d45`, PR #127)

Both directions closed for non-default brands, by separating the two facts the
single slug comparison had been doing double duty for: whether the card channel
is live (the brand's switch, §11.10) and whether this is the default brand
(which decides the MarleyMoves Ltd disclosure, §3.5).

- `emailTheme` derives `cardPhone` from `brand.cardPaymentsEnabled` instead of
  an `opts` flag no caller ever set. The flag survives as an override for a
  caller that knows the global kill switch is down.
- `cardPaymentsAvailable(sb, brandSlug)` ANDs the brand switch with the global
  one, closing direction B. `startCardPayment` had to load the quote BEFORE the
  check, because the brand rides on the quote — checking first is precisely what
  made the switch inert on `/q`.
- `invoicePayClause()` replaces the two slug-keyed ternaries in `accept-flow`. A
  card-enabled non-default brand now gets BOTH the card mention (on its own
  number, never Marley's — that number reaches a different office) and the
  disclosure; a card-off brand gets neither.
- `depositStepCopy` reads the theme rather than an unset `offerCard`.
- A brand row that cannot be read is not a brand with card on: failing open
  would put a card button on a surface whose copy says bank-only.

## Known remainder — NOT fixed, deliberately

**Marley's own toggle still governs nothing in the copy.** This is the
suggested-cause's second option ("remove the control until it does something")
left open rather than taken.

`tests/lib/comms/email-brand.test.ts` byte-locks the default theme to be
independent of the brands row — *"marley, absent and null all yield the
identical literal theme"* — which is the single-brand invariant written as a
test. PRD §11.10 says both switches govern every brand. The two disagree, and
the disagreement is only theoretical today because the Marley row seeds `true`
(confirmed in `0104_brands.sql` itself, not inferred).

Resolving it means either loosening that byte-lock or hiding the toggle for the
default brand. Neither is a call to make by quietly rewriting a test to fit a
change, so it is Peter's. `invoicePayClause` was made literal for the default
brand for the same reason, so the two surfaces at least agree with each other
rather than the toggle being half-live.
